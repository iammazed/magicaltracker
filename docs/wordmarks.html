Six ways to set the wordmark

“Magical” in a script face, “Tracker” in Figtree semibold — the same sans already running on the site. Each candidate is shown at logo scale and at header scale, on the light ground and on the twilight gradient, because the mark has to hold in both.

The constraint: none of these may read as the Disney script. That mark is spiky, angular and signature-like, with a distinctive looping capital. Everything below is deliberately round, brushy or vintage-travel instead — adjacent in feeling, clearly separate in form. Waltograph and its lookalikes are off the table entirely.

The candidates

Pacifico

Recommendation

Round, warm and thick enough to survive being shrunk to a favicon. The roundness is what puts real distance between it and the Disney script, which is all sharp angles. Widely used, but used because it works, and nothing about it says “theme park” by itself — your palette does that job.

Grand Hotel

Vintage travel

Reads like a 1930s luggage label, which is genuinely on-concept for a passport app. More elegant and less cartoonish than Pacifico, with thinner strokes — the trade-off is that it loses definition faster at small sizes, so it wants a slightly larger minimum.

Berkshire Swash

Storybook

Not a true script — an upright display face with decorative swashes. The most distinctive option here, and the least likely to be mistaken for anyone else’s mark. Its upright stance also makes it the most legible of the whimsical options at small sizes.

Courgette

Friendly, restrained

The quietest of the six. A brush-ish italic that feels handwritten without demanding attention, which makes it the safest choice if you ever worry the mark is competing with the rest of the interface. Less memorable as a result.

Yellowtail

Retro brush

A flowing brush script with strong contrast between thick and thin strokes. Energetic and nostalgic. Worth a careful look though — of the six, its slant and connected flow sit closest to signature-style scripts, so it is the one I would want an attorney to glance at.

Caveat Bold

Handwritten

Actual handwriting rather than a formal script. Casual, personal, and it suits the “made by one person” framing on your support page. The most informal option, and the one most likely to read as a note rather than a brand.

What I would pick

Pacifico, with Berkshire Swash as the runner-up. Pacifico is warm and unmistakably friendly, survives being scaled down to a favicon or an app icon, and its roundness is the clearest possible signal that you are not imitating anyone. Berkshire Swash is more distinctive and legally the safest, but it is a louder mark that you would have to live with everywhere.

All six are Google Fonts, free for commercial use under the SIL Open Font License, so any of them can ship in the app, on the site, and in your App Store artwork.
